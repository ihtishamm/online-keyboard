Your phone can already type Urdu. Android phones with Gboard (Google's keyboard) and every iPhone include an Urdu keyboard: you only have to turn it on. This guide shows how, and how to choose between typing Urdu letters directly and typing in English letters that turn into Urdu.

Phone menus differ between makers and software versions, so the names below may not match your screen word for word. When in doubt, look for "Languages" inside your keyboard's settings.

## Android: add Urdu to Gboard

Most Android phones use Gboard. If yours uses another keyboard (for example Samsung Keyboard), you can install Gboard from the Play Store, or look for a similar "Languages" setting in your current keyboard.

1. Open any app where you can type, such as Messages, so the keyboard appears.
2. Tap the **gear** icon on the keyboard to open Gboard settings. You can also go to the phone's **Settings → System → Languages & input → On-screen keyboard → Gboard**.
3. Tap **Languages**, then **Add keyboard**.
4. Search for **Urdu** and tap it.
5. Gboard shows the Urdu keyboard styles to choose from. Pick one and tap **Done**.

### Urdu letters or English letters?

Gboard offers two ways to type Urdu, shown as separate choices when you add the language:

- **Urdu letters.** A keyboard with the Urdu alphabet on its keys. Good if you know where the letters are, or are happy to learn.
- **Transliteration**, usually labelled with **abc** or "Urdu (abc → اردو)". You type with English letters, as you would in a chat (`kya haal hai`), and Gboard suggests the Urdu words (<span lang="ur">کیا حال ہے</span>) above the keys. Tap a suggestion, or press space to take the first one.

If you already write Roman Urdu on WhatsApp, transliteration is the quickest way to start. You can add both and switch between them.

## iPhone and iPad: add the Urdu keyboard

1. Open **Settings** and tap **General**.
2. Tap **Keyboard**, then **Keyboards**.
3. Tap **Add New Keyboard…** and choose **Urdu** from the list.

The iPhone keyboard types Urdu letters directly. It does not convert English letters into Urdu. If you want to type Roman Urdu and get Urdu script on an iPhone, use the [Roman Urdu keyboard](/urdu/roman) in Safari, or a third-party keyboard app from the App Store. Before you allow "Full Access" for any third-party keyboard, remember that it can see everything you type.

## Switch between English and Urdu

- **Gboard:** tap the **globe** key next to the space bar. If there is no globe key, press and hold the space bar and choose a language.
- **iPhone:** tap the **globe** key below the letters to move to the next keyboard. Press and hold it to pick from a list.

The keyboard remembers your last choice, so it stays in Urdu until you switch back.

## Find letters that are not on the main keys

Urdu has more letters than fit on a phone keyboard. On both Gboard and the iPhone, related letters hide behind a long press. For example, press and hold <span lang="ur">ا</span> for <span lang="ur">آ</span>, or <span lang="ur">ن</span> for <span lang="ur">ں</span>. Other letters, numbers and punctuation such as <span lang="ur">۔</span> and <span lang="ur">؟</span> are on the second page of keys.

## Typing a long piece of Urdu on a phone

A phone keyboard is fine for messages. For something longer, such as a letter, a post or a school assignment, a bigger screen helps:

- The [online Urdu keyboard](/urdu) works in your phone's browser with its own on-screen keys, so you do not need to change any settings. It saves as you go, so you can close the tab and come back.
- When you are done, use **Share** to send the text to WhatsApp or another app, or **Download** to save it as a Word file or a picture.
- Text appears in Nastaliq, the style Urdu readers expect, even where your phone's own font is Naskh.

## Troubleshooting

**Urdu is not in Gboard's list.** Update Gboard from the Play Store, then try again. Older versions offered fewer languages.

**I added transliteration but suggestions are in English.** Check that the keyboard is set to the Urdu (abc) option, not English. Tap the globe key to switch.

**The text shows as boxes in an app.** The app's font has no Urdu letters. The text is still correct: copy it into another app and it will show properly.
